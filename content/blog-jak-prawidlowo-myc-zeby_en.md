# Brushing is not enough. How can you really look after a healthy smile?

Most of us know that we should brush our teeth morning and evening. Yet tooth decay, bleeding gums and tartar buildup remain common problems. Why? Because effective daily oral hygiene depends not only on consistency but also on technique and choosing suitable tools.

The good news is that effective oral care does not have to be complicated. A few simple habits can make a real difference.

## Start with the areas that are easiest to overlook

When brushing, we usually focus on the front surfaces of our teeth. However, bacterial plaque also accumulates along the gumline, on the inner surfaces and between the teeth.

Even an electric toothbrush cannot always clean the spaces between teeth thoroughly. That is why it is worth adding dental floss or appropriately sized interdental brushes to your daily routine.

This is particularly important for people with crowns, bridges or orthodontic appliances, where additional cleaning tools may be needed.

## Two minutes, twice a day

Brush your teeth at least twice a day for around two minutes, using fluoride toothpaste.

There is no need to press hard. Brushing too forcefully can irritate the gums and contribute to wear of the tooth surface.

The important thing is to clean all surfaces thoroughly. Both manual and electric toothbrushes can work well when used correctly. Choose the type that best suits your needs and dexterity.

After brushing, spit out excess toothpaste but avoid rinsing your mouth vigorously with water. This allows fluoride to remain on the teeth for longer.

## What about morning coffee and acidic drinks?

Sweets are not the only dietary concern for enamel. Acidic drinks, fruit juices and frequent snacking also affect conditions in the mouth.

After consuming acidic foods or drinks, it is better to wait before brushing rather than brushing immediately. You can rinse your mouth with water in the meantime.

It is also worth reducing the frequency of sugary snacks. What matters to your teeth is not only the amount of sugar but how often bacteria are supplied with fuel to produce acids.

## Can mouthwash replace brushing?

No. Mouthwash can complement oral hygiene, but it does not physically remove plaque in the same way as brushing and cleaning between the teeth.

Not every mouthwash is suitable for prolonged daily use. Therapeutic products, including those containing chlorhexidine, should be used according to a dentist's advice.

## Why does tartar require a dental appointment?

Soft plaque can be removed by daily brushing. Once it mineralises into tartar, however, an ordinary toothbrush can no longer remove it.

Professional hygiene treatments, including scaling and air polishing, can help. How often they are needed depends on individual circumstances, the rate of tartar buildup and gum health.

## Five habits worth remembering

- Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste.
- Clean between your teeth every day.
- Avoid pressing too hard with your toothbrush.
- Limit frequent snacking and sipping sugary drinks.
- Have your teeth and gums checked regularly by a dentist.

## When should you book a dental appointment?

You do not need to wait for toothache. Bleeding gums, persistent bad breath, sensitivity or visible tartar are all reasons to seek advice.
