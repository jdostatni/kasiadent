# Root canal treatment without unnecessary stress. What should you know before your appointment?

The words “root canal treatment” make many patients anxious. Common questions include whether the procedure will hurt, how long it will take and whether they will be able to return to their usual activities afterwards.

Modern endodontics is different from the treatment some people remember from years ago. Local anaesthesia, accurate diagnosis and magnification make it possible to work precisely while taking care of the patient's comfort.

## What causes pain in a tooth that needs root canal treatment?

Inside each tooth is the pulp, tissue containing nerves and blood vessels. When it becomes inflamed or infected, severe pain may develop.

Patients describe throbbing, pain that becomes worse in the evening, sensitivity to heat or difficulty biting down.

Importantly, a tooth that needs root canal treatment does not always hurt. Sometimes changes develop without obvious symptoms and are only discovered during an examination or X-ray.

## Does root canal treatment hurt?

The procedure is usually carried out under local anaesthesia to reduce pain.

During treatment, you may feel touch, pressure or vibration from the instruments, but you should not experience sharp pain. Tell your dentist straight away if you feel uncomfortable.

With severe inflammation, achieving effective anaesthesia may be more difficult. The dentist can then use additional anaesthetic techniques or adjust the treatment approach.

No procedure can be guaranteed to be completely free of discomfort in every case. However, appropriate anaesthesia allows most patients to undergo treatment comfortably.

## What happens during microscope-assisted root canal treatment?

The first step is an examination to determine whether the tooth is suitable for endodontic treatment. The dentist may take an X-ray to assess the roots and surrounding tissues.

After administering anaesthesia and isolating the treatment area, the dentist accesses the root canal system.

Diseased pulp is removed, the canals are cleaned and disinfected, and then they are sealed with filling material.

A dental microscope provides a magnified view of the treatment area. It is particularly useful for locating narrow canals, assessing canal openings and managing more complex cases that require precision.

Finally, the tooth needs to be restored appropriately to recover its function and reduce the risk of fracture.

## Can a tooth hurt after root canal treatment?

Yes. Temporary tenderness, particularly when biting, may occur for a few days after the procedure and usually gradually subsides.

However, if pain is severe or increasing, or if you develop swelling, fever or difficulty opening your mouth, contact your dentist. These symptoms need assessment rather than simply waiting for them to pass.

## One appointment or several?

There is no single answer for every patient. Treatment time depends on the number of canals, their anatomy, the extent of infection and whether this is initial treatment or retreatment.

Some teeth can be treated in one appointment; others require several visits.

The dentist will discuss the plan after assessing your individual case.

## Can root canal treatment save your natural tooth?

Endodontic treatment aims to remove infection from the root canal system so that the tooth can continue to function.

Not every tooth can be saved. Extensive damage to the crown, a root fracture or unfavourable anatomy may limit treatment options.

That is why individual diagnosis and assessment of the prognosis are so important.
